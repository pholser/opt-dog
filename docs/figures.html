Reading these numbers

  • Times are planned times at the judges' rates. Published programs are re-timed the same way, so they show what was planned, not what happened on the day.
  • Group and Best in Show durations are upper bounds, so finish times run late. Differences between schedules are more reliable than clock times.
  • Scores cover conformation only on both sides. Obedience and rally run in their own rings, outside the optimizer.
  • Hard-rule counts leave out V1, a judge over the daily entry limit, which is an input problem no schedule can fix.
  • Exhibitor conflicts are left out because they rest on synthetic entries.
  • The optimizer gives up to 10 minutes of finish to avoid a short block. 3a and 3b are the earlier steps: 3b chooses each judge's group order, and 3c also packs the breeds inside each run into full blocks.
  • "Proven optimal" means nothing better exists, counting 10 minutes per short block, under 3c's own rules: the block cap, the time grid, each judge keeping their groups together, and the packed breed order. A schedule with a freer order could still beat it.
